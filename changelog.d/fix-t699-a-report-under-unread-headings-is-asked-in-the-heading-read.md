### Fixed

- **An answer whose claims open under headings the claim reader does not read is asked in the heading it reads (T-699).** When a tool loop's last message writes claim field lines (`EVIDENCE:`, `CONFIDENCE:`, `TECHNIQUE:`) and no heading the claim grammar reads, the one final-answer nudge states the heading the platform reads, `CLAIM <n>: <sentence>`, and an example whose sentence is a placeholder, from the same constants the grammar is tested against. A report read in part, a claim of it begun under a heading the grammar does not read, is asked the same turn; its reply replaces it only when it begins at least as many claims and leaves none unread. It is the same single turn; a message with no field line gets the plain nudge.
- **A numbered claim heading whose emphasis and note stand in either order before its delimiter is read (T-699).** `**CLAIM 1** (revises claim 2). …`, `**CLAIM 1 (revised).** …` and `CLAIM 1 (revised). …` open a claim with its number and its note, as `**CLAIM 1 (revised):** …` does: a numbered heading's note may follow the emphasis that closes its label, and a dot after a numbered heading's note is its delimiter. A dot after a heading with no note or no number, labels other than `CLAIM` (`C1`, `R1`) and prose open no claim, and under a DISPUTES section a heading ended by a dot is not counted and does not end the section. Every claim read before is read as it was, and a line costs time linear in its length.
- **A claim written whole under a numbered heading ended by a dot with no note is counted as begun and not read (T-699).** `CLAIM 3. …` or `**CLAIM 3.** …` with a claim field line as its next written line is no claim the reader reads, and it is counted against the claims read: the shortfall sentence and the cut-answer question name it. With prose beneath it, it is prose.
